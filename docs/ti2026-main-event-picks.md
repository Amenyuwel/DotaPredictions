## One rule explains most of the reasoning

Your fantasy score for each slot is **the best single series that role plays** — not the
total of everything they do. A series is scored by its two best games.

That one rule changes what a good pick looks like:

- **Getting knocked out early doesn't kill a slot.** A team eliminated after two series
  still keeps the better of those two. You lose *chances* at a big series, not the points
  you already banked.
- **Consistency beats a soft schedule.** A player who reliably posts big games on a
  doomed team is worth more than a streaky player on a safe one.
- **A deep run helps, but less than you'd think.** More series means more attempts at a
  good one, with heavy diminishing returns.

## Coach Titles: keep what you have

Titles are the biggest free lever you have left — worth **+6.6%** versus running none.

**Suffix: `the Clutch`.** Clear winner. It pays on the last possible game of a series, and
since a series is scored on its two best games, a boosted decider is also more likely to
be one of them. Worth roughly twice what the next-best suffix gives you.

Two suffixes look tempting and aren't:

- **`the Underdog`** fires more often than anything else on the list (34% of games) and is
  nearly worthless — it pays when you *lose*, and only your best series counts.
- **`the Decisive`** advertises +24% and delivers under +1%. Games under 25 minutes are
  about 2% of the sample, and short games suppress almost everything your banners score.

**Prefix: `Cerulean` is fine.** It's the top scorer for this roster, mostly because Nisha
plays blue heroes more than anyone else in the field. Crimson and Heroic are within 0.2%,
which is nothing, so switching is optional rather than an upgrade.

I did check whether the ban phase could quietly kill Cerulean by removing the blue heroes
it depends on. It doesn't — accounting for who gets banned actually nudges its value *up*.

## Four worries I checked and can set aside

**"If Liquid get knocked out, Nisha's slot is dead."** No. You keep the best series he
already played. Being eliminated early costs a slot between 3% and 7% — real, but small.

**"PARIVISION and Spirit are both my picks and they'll fight each other."** They do meet
59% of the time, usually in Upper Round 2 — where the loser drops to the lower bracket
rather than going out. Across the whole roster this changes your expected score by
**0.0%**. Picking around the bracket isn't worth doing.

**"Bans will wreck my hero-based prefix."** TI's ban phase is savage at the hero level —
Treant Protector is banned in 93% of games — but it moves your projection by about
**0.1%**. Your players' contested heroes aren't the ones they score most on.

**"Some of these teams never played each other — how can you predict them?"** Through
common opponents. If Falcons beat teams that Liquid struggle with, that comparison holds
even without a direct meeting, which matters because **four pairs in this bracket have
never met** — including Iron Wing vs PARIVISION and PARIVISION vs Yandex, both of which could
happen in the upper bracket.

I also tested the opposite approach: trusting direct head-to-head records instead. **It
predicts worse.** On 298 games the model had never seen, adding head-to-head made every
prediction worse the more I leaned on it, and relying on it alone was worse than a coin
flip. Small records are mostly luck — your own example shows why. Falcons and Liquid have
met 15 times and split them almost exactly (8-7), so their head-to-head says "even" while
their recent form says Falcons are slightly better. The form is the more reliable signal:
the model gives Falcons 56.6%, and using the head-to-head record barely moves it (55.6%).

## What went into these numbers

Not an exhaustive list, but the things that actually moved the answer:

**Match history, group stage included.** Six months of professional matches, with all 109
games of TI's group stage in there and counting most heavily because they're the most
recent. That's what puts PARIVISION top of the field — the 4-0 run is doing real work in
these numbers. Around 1,300 replays read in detail. Player identities pinned by hand,
because team names change (BetBoom play as BoomBoys, PARIVISION as TEAM VISION) and
searching a nickname globally returns the wrong player.

**Team strength rather than head-to-head.** Every result feeds one strength number per
team, so any two teams can be compared even if they never met. I tested adding direct
head-to-head records on top and it made predictions worse, so it's deliberately left out.

**Corrections to the raw stats.** Longer games hand out more of almost everything, so
every stat line is put on a common footing and then restated at TI's actual game length —
45 minutes, not the 40 the maths defaults to. Stat lines are also adjusted for how strong
the opposition was, and games against weak opposition in minor leagues count for less.

**The exact scoring rules.** Per-game banner stats, averaged across the two players in a
duo, best two games in a series, best series in the period. Transcribed from the client's
own HOW TO PLAY page and cross-checked.

**Your actual banners.** All five emblems per slot, with their quality tiers and traits,
including the ones that don't pay out: two of your emblems need every quality on the
banner to be different, and yours aren't, so they're worth less than they look.

**Coach Titles, applied game by game.** Prefixes depend on the hero played, using Valve's
own hero classifications pulled from the game files, so a prefix is worth what *your
players'* hero choices make it worth — not a league average.

**The drawn bracket.** Real matchups, Bo3 throughout with a Bo5 Grand Final, and each
team's realistic path through it.

**The draft.** Every pick and ban from 993 matches, so heroes that get banned out count
for less.

**Honest error bars.** Every projection has a margin of error attached, and where two
options fall inside it I say they're tied instead of ranking them.

## What could still go wrong

- **I might be scoring one stat the wrong way.** Teamfight Participation is the single
  biggest chunk of the support score, and I've assumed it pays out proportionally. If the
  real formula is stepped, that ranking shifts.
- **One emblem is invisible to me.** Lotuses aren't in any public data, so your support
  banner has an emblem I score as zero. It isn't zero in the game.
- **Small samples.** Some duos have only 40 games on record. Nigma's cores especially are
  probably underrated for that reason.
- **No matchup styles.** Each team is a single strength number, so if one team genuinely
  has another's number for stylistic reasons, I can't see it. I tested head-to-head
  records as a way in and they predicted worse, but "worse on average" isn't "never true
  in a specific case".
- **Rosters and reality.** Stand-ins, illness, a patch mid-event — none of that is
  modelled.
- **27.5% still means 72.5% someone else wins.** PARIVISION are the most likely champion,
  not a safe bet. Falcons at 17.9% are entirely live.
